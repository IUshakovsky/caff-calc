How the estimates are produced

The calculator's outputs are estimates derived from published population-level guidelines, not personal medical guidance. It combines a body-weight calculation with an age-band ceiling and adjusts for pregnancy, breastfeeding and a self-reported sensitivity setting.

The underlying reference points are:

  • The U.S. Food & Drug Administration, which cites 400 mg per day as an amount not generally associated with negative effects in healthy adults.
  • The European Food Safety Authority, whose 2015 opinion assesses single doses of 3 mg per kg of body weight and habitual daily intakes of 5.7 mg per kg as not raising safety concerns for healthy adults, and which addresses children and adolescents on a per-kilogram basis.
  • The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists, which describes moderate caffeine consumption during pregnancy as under 200 mg per day.

These are population figures. Individual tolerance varies substantially — genetics, liver function, smoking status, pregnancy and several common medications all change how quickly caffeine is cleared. A result the calculator describes as within limits may still be too much for you.

Intended users

This tool is designed for adults. The guidance it applies to anyone under 18 is deliberately more restrictive than the adult figures:

Under 13

The calculator does not return a "safe" caffeine allowance for children. No health authority recommends caffeine for young children; the European safety guidance for children and teens is set per kilogram of body weight.

Ages 13–17

A substantially lower ceiling is applied than for adults, calculated on a per-kilogram basis.

Parents and guardians should treat these outputs as a conversation starter with a paediatrician, not as permission.
