## A flat 40 mg, whatever the size

Starbucks lists 40 mg of caffeine for hot Earl Grey Tea at every size it sells, Short through Venti, matching its [English Breakfast Tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-english-breakfast-tea/' | relative_url }}) exactly, both being black tea bases. Like the rest of Starbucks' brewed hot teas, the dose comes from a single bag, so a bigger cup means more hot water around the same bag rather than more caffeine.

## The tea behind the London Fog

This is the tea Starbucks builds its [London Fog Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-london-fog-latte/' | relative_url }}) from, steamed with milk and vanilla syrup, and that drink carries the same 40 mg at every size as the plain tea it starts from.

Against home brewing, 40 mg runs a little below USDA's figure for [black tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/black-tea/' | relative_url }}) steeped at home, 47 mg for an 8 oz cup, likely because a café bag steeps for less time than a fully brewed pot.

Bergamot oil, the ingredient that distinguishes Earl Grey from a plain black tea, doesn't add or remove caffeine; the leaf underneath is the same type of black tea Starbucks uses for its English Breakfast blend, just flavoured differently. Choosing between the two at the counter is entirely a matter of taste, not caffeine, since both publish the identical 40 mg figure.

## Where it fits in a day

At 40 mg, even a Venti is far under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Ten Ventis, 400 mg, would be needed to reach the daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, five cups still fit under the day's allowance.
