## A little lighter than plain milk chocolate

USDA measures M&M's Milk Chocolate at 14 mg of caffeine per 100 g, which comes to about 7 mg in a standard 1.69 oz (48 g) pack. That's a bit below USDA's generic figure for [milk chocolate]({{ '/caffeine-in/milk-chocolate/' | relative_url }}) (20 mg per 100 g), likely because the candy shell and any fillings dilute the chocolate content per gram compared to a solid bar.

## Negligible next to coffee or dark chocolate

A whole pack's 7 mg is a fraction of an 8 oz cup of [drip coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/brewed-coffee/' | relative_url }}) at USDA's reference strength (95 mg), and far below an ounce of [dark chocolate]({{ '/caffeine-in/dark-chocolate-70-85/' | relative_url }}) (about 23 mg), which has much more cocoa solids per gram.

## Other M&M's varieties differ

This figure is specific to the Milk Chocolate variety. Peanut, pretzel, or dark chocolate M&M's have different ratios of chocolate to filling, which changes the caffeine per pack even at the same total weight; a filled candy generally carries less caffeine per gram than a solid chocolate piece, since the filling itself contributes none.

## Where it fits in a day

At 7 mg, a pack is negligible against the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults; dozens of packs would be needed to approach it. It matters most for children, whose smaller daily allowances make even a low-caffeine snack a slightly larger share of the day, and it's a reminder that the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) notes naturally occurring caffeine, like the kind in chocolate, doesn't have to be listed separately on a nutrition label.
