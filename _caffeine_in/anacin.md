## The lightest dose among the common OTC options

Anacin combines aspirin with a modest 32 mg of caffeine per tablet, according to its drug label; the standard two-tablet dose carries 64 mg, less than a third of an 8 oz cup of [drip coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/brewed-coffee/' | relative_url }}) at USDA's reference strength (95 mg). That is noticeably lighter than [Excedrin]({{ '/caffeine-in/excedrin-extra-strength/' | relative_url }}) (65 mg a caplet) or [Midol Complete]({{ '/caffeine-in/midol-complete/' | relative_url }}) (60 mg a gelcap), the two other common over-the-counter products that pair caffeine with a pain reliever.

## Why caffeine is in a pain reliever at all

Caffeine is included in products like Anacin as a pain reliever aid, on the theory that it improves the effectiveness of aspirin and similar drugs rather than acting as a stimulant in its own right. At 64 mg a dose, it's a small enough amount that the boost, if any, comes without much of the alertness effect a full cup of coffee brings.

## Counting it alongside everything else

Even though 64 mg is a small dose on its own, it's easy to overlook when it's part of a pain reliever rather than a drink. Someone taking a dose of Anacin on top of a normal day's coffee and tea should still add it to the running total against the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults, small as it is.

## Where it fits against a single dose

A two-tablet dose, 64 mg, is far under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults, and under a third of the 200 mg daily ceiling [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses in pregnancy. Check with a doctor before taking any aspirin-containing product during pregnancy, regardless of the caffeine amount.
