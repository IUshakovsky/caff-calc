## No cocoa solids, no caffeine

White chocolate is made from cocoa butter, sugar and milk solids, but not the cocoa solids that give milk and dark chocolate their caffeine. USDA's figure for white chocolate is 0 mg per 100 g, a genuine zero, unlike [milk chocolate]({{ '/caffeine-in/milk-chocolate/' | relative_url }}) (20 mg per 100 g) or any of the [dark chocolate]({{ '/caffeine-in/dark-chocolate-70-85/' | relative_url }}) bands, which range from 43 to 86 mg per 100 g depending on cacao percentage.

## Why the mocha with its name isn't zero

Starbucks' [White Chocolate Mocha]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-white-chocolate-mocha/' | relative_url }}) still carries 150 mg of caffeine in a Grande, the same as a plain latte, because that number comes entirely from the espresso shots, not the white chocolate sauce. The white chocolate itself contributes nothing to the total, which is exactly why that drink matches a plain latte's caffeine instead of running higher, the way the regular Caffè Mocha does with real chocolate.

## Technically not "chocolate" by some definitions

White chocolate is made from cocoa butter, the fat extracted from cocoa beans, without the cocoa solids that carry both flavour compounds and caffeine. Some food regulators and purists argue it shouldn't be called chocolate at all for exactly this reason; whatever the label debate, the caffeine picture is unambiguous: there isn't any.

## Where it fits in a day

At 0 mg, white chocolate adds nothing to any daily total, including the 400 mg the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults or the 200 mg daily ceiling [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses in pregnancy. It's a genuinely safe chocolate choice for children or anyone avoiding caffeine, unlike milk or dark chocolate, which still carry a small amount.
