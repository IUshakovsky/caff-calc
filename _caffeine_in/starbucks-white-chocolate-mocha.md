## Same as a latte, unlike its chocolate mocha sibling

A Grande Starbucks White Chocolate Mocha carries 150 mg of caffeine: 75 mg in a Short or Tall, 150 mg in a Grande or Venti, exactly matching the hot [Caffè Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caffe-latte/' | relative_url }}) at every size. That is a useful contrast with the regular [Caffè Mocha]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caffe-mocha/' | relative_url }}) (175 mg in a Grande), which runs higher because dark and milk chocolate carry a little caffeine of their own. [White chocolate]({{ '/caffeine-in/white-chocolate/' | relative_url }}) contains none, by USDA's figures, so swapping the sauce removes that small addition and leaves the drink's caffeine exactly where the two espresso shots put it.

## What sets the dose

Because the white chocolate sauce contributes nothing, every size of this drink tracks the shot count alone: one shot for Short and Tall, two for Grande and Venti. The sweetness comes from sugar and vanilla notes in the sauce, not anything that changes the caffeine math.

Anyone tracking caffeine closely can treat this drink as functionally identical to a plain latte, which makes it a safe stand-in when the sweeter flavour is wanted without changing the dose from a known baseline.

## Where it fits in a day

At 150 mg, a Grande sits under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Two Grandes, 300 mg, leave 100 mg of the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, a Grande uses most of the day's allowance on its own.
