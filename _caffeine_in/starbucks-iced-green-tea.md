## Matches the iced black tea exactly

Starbucks' Iced Green Tea carries 20 to 25 mg in a Tall, 25 to 30 mg in a Grande, 40 to 45 mg in a Venti, and 55 to 60 mg in a Trenta, the identical range published for its [Iced Black Tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-iced-black-tea/' | relative_url }}) at every size. Both are brewed and served over ice at a similar strength, so the leaf type doesn't change the dose much here, even though home-brewed [green tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/green-tea/' | relative_url }}) generally runs lower than black tea (about 29 mg against 47 mg per 8 oz cup by USDA's figures).

## Much lighter than matcha

This steeped-and-strained iced green tea carries far less caffeine than Starbucks' [Iced Matcha Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-iced-matcha-latte/' | relative_url }}) (65 mg in a Grande), because matcha is whisked whole-leaf powder rather than brewed and discarded tea leaves, which pulls much more caffeine into the cup.

For anyone choosing between the two mainly on caffeine grounds, it doesn't matter which one you pick; the choice between iced green and iced black tea at Starbucks comes down entirely to flavour, since neither publishes a lower figure than the other at any of the four sizes.

## Where it fits in a day

At up to 30 mg, a Grande is far under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Even a Trenta at the top of its range, 60 mg, is a small fraction of the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, several Grandes a day still fit comfortably.
