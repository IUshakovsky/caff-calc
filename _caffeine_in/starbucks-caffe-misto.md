## Half coffee, half milk, half the caffeine

A Caffè Misto is brewed coffee cut with steamed milk in roughly equal parts, Starbucks' answer to a French-style café au lait. Starbucks lists 75 mg of caffeine in a Short or Tall, 150 mg in a Grande, and 195 mg in a Venti. Because the cup is half milk, a Grande Misto carries less than half the caffeine of a Grande [Pike Place Roast]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-pike-place-roast/' | relative_url }}) (315 to 390 mg), even though both start from the same brewed coffee.

## Compared to a latte

Ounce for ounce, a Misto's caffeine is close to a Grande [Caffè Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caffe-latte/' | relative_url }}) (150 mg), even though the two drinks are built differently: a latte's caffeine comes from two shots of espresso, a Misto's from brewed coffee diluted with milk. The two land on the same number in a Grande mostly by coincidence of Starbucks' recipes, not because the drinks work the same way.

Requesting decaf coffee in the mix turns a Misto into a low-caffeine drink, closer to a [decaf coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/decaf-coffee/' | relative_url }}) with a splash of milk.

Ordering a Misto is also a simple way to stretch a smaller amount of brewed coffee across a bigger, milkier cup without asking for a specific espresso drink by name.

## Where it fits in a day

At 150 mg, a Grande Misto sits under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults, and two of them, 300 mg, leave 100 mg of the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, a Grande uses most of the day's allowance on its own.
