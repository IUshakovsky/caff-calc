## USDA's reference figure for a can of cola

USDA measures regular carbonated cola at 9 mg of caffeine per 100 g, which comes to about 33 mg in a standard 12 fl oz can, 44 mg in a 16 fl oz bottle and 89 mg in a 32 fl oz large fountain drink. Because the figure scales with volume, a gas-station or movie-theater fountain cola can carry two or three times a can's caffeine simply by being poured bigger, with nothing about the recipe changing.

This generic figure sits close to named brands rather than standing apart from them. [Coca-Cola]({{ '/caffeine-in/coca-cola-classic/' | relative_url }}) carries 34 mg per 12 oz can by the Coca-Cola Company's own figures, just above USDA's generic number, and [Pepsi]({{ '/caffeine-in/pepsi/' | relative_url }}) carries 38 mg. [Diet Pepsi]({{ '/caffeine-in/diet-pepsi/' | relative_url }}), at 35 mg, is closer still. A generic store-brand cola without its own published figure is reasonably estimated from USDA's number if the label doesn't list caffeine directly.

## Fountain sizes change the math

A can and a bottle are close enough in caffeine per ounce that swapping between them barely matters, but fountain sizes break that pattern. A 32 oz large fountain cola at 89 mg carries more caffeine than a can and a bottle combined, and gets there in one cup with a single price tag and no serving-size cue on the container the way a can provides. It's also closer to a full cup of [drip coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/brewed-coffee/' | relative_url }}) (95 mg at USDA's reference strength) than most people expect from a soda.

## Where it fits

At USDA's 33 mg per can, twelve cans stay under the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults, well past what anyone would actually drink in a day. The large fountain size is the one to watch: two of them, 178 mg, already approach half that daily figure, and in pregnancy a single 32 oz fountain cola uses close to half the 200 mg daily ceiling [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses.
