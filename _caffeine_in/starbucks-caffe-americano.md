## Espresso and hot water

A Caffè Americano is espresso shots topped with hot water. The result is a drink that is similar in volume to brewed coffee but has the flavour profile and caffeine load of espresso. Starbucks builds the cup by adding one 75 mg espresso shot for each size step, so the caffeine scales predictably with the cup.

The published Starbucks nutrition gives 75 mg for a Short (8 fl oz), 150 mg for a Tall (12 fl oz), 225 mg for a Grande (16 fl oz) and 300 mg for a Venti (20 fl oz). That means a Venti Americano contains four espresso shots, the same espresso count as a hot Venti Caffè Latte.

## Why it matters for counting

Because the drink is mostly water, it is easy to underestimate the caffeine. It tastes lighter than a latte of the same size but can carry the same espresso dose. A Grande Americano at 225 mg is more than half of the 400 mg daily figure the FDA cites for healthy adults, and a Venti reaches three-quarters of that amount in a single cup.

If you are comparing against [drip coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/brewed-coffee/' | relative_url }}), a Grande Americano lands close to a 12 oz home-brewed mug, while a Venti Americano is roughly equivalent to two small cups of brewed coffee.

## Why the name is misleading

The name "Americano" comes from the Italian practice of diluting espresso with hot water to approximate the strength of American-style brewed coffee. The caffeine, however, does not dilute. Espresso is already highly concentrated, and adding water only changes the volume and mouthfeel. A Venti Americano still contains four full espresso shots, so treating it like a large cup of weak filter coffee is a common way to overshoot your daily total.
