## The same shots as a latte, more foam

A Starbucks Cappuccino is built from the same shot counts as its hot [Caffè Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caffe-latte/' | relative_url }}): one shot (75 mg) in a Short or Tall, two shots (150 mg) in a Grande or Venti. The two drinks differ in the ratio of foam to steamed milk, not in caffeine; a cappuccino just tastes stronger because there's less milk diluting each sip.

That also means, unusually among Starbucks sizes, a cappuccino doesn't get any stronger between Grande and Venti: both carry 150 mg, the extra volume in the Venti going to foam rather than another shot.

## Compared to home cappuccino

USDA's generic figure for a [home-style cappuccino]({{ '/caffeine-in/cappuccino/' | relative_url }}), one shot with steamed milk in a smaller cup, runs close to a single Starbucks shot too. The main difference between chains and home brewing is cup size and shot strength, not the basic one-or-two-shot structure, and that holds whether the milk is steamed into a thick microfoam or a lighter one, since foam style changes texture, not caffeine.

That also means ordering "small" for the caffeine and "large" for the milk-to-foam ratio isn't possible the way it is with a latte: past Grande, the drink just gets frothier, not stronger.

## Where it fits in a day

At 150 mg, a Grande or Venti cappuccino sits under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Two Grandes, 300 mg, leave 100 mg of the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, a Short or Tall at 75 mg leaves the most room.
