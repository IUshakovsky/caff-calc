## Not the caffeine-free option it looks like

Starbucks' Hot Chocolate carries 15 mg of caffeine in a Short, 20 mg in a Tall, 25 mg in a Grande, and 35 mg in a Venti, from the cocoa in the mocha sauce. That's a modest dose, well below any of Starbucks' coffee drinks, but worth knowing before handing one to a child expecting a caffeine-free treat: a Grande carries exactly as much caffeine as a Grande [Decaf Pike Place Roast]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-decaf-pike-place-roast/' | relative_url }}) (25 mg).

## In line with cocoa generally

This lands close to what a homemade hot chocolate made with real [cocoa powder]({{ '/caffeine-in/cocoa-powder/' | relative_url }}) would carry, and above USDA's generic figure for [hot chocolate made with milk]({{ '/caffeine-in/hot-chocolate/' | relative_url }}) (about 7 mg for an 8 oz cup), likely reflecting a stronger mocha sauce than a typical powdered mix.

## Why cocoa carries caffeine at all

Cocoa naturally contains a small amount of caffeine alongside theobromine, a related but milder stimulant compound that's more abundant in chocolate than caffeine is. Both come from the same cacao bean, which is why any genuinely chocolate-based drink, hot chocolate included, is never quite the caffeine-free option it's sometimes assumed to be.

## Where it fits in a day

At 25 mg, a Grande is a small fraction of the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults, and of the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites. Even in pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, several cups a day fit comfortably. It matters most as a reminder that "hot chocolate" and "caffeine-free" aren't the same thing.
