## The same caffeine as regular

Diet Mountain Dew carries 54 mg of caffeine in a 12 fl oz can, exactly matching regular [Mountain Dew]({{ '/caffeine-in/mountain-dew/' | relative_url }}) at the same size. Caffeine and sugar are added separately, so removing the sugar for a diet formula doesn't touch the caffeine; this is a pattern that holds across most diet sodas, not just Mountain Dew.

At 54 mg, it remains one of the more caffeinated mainstream sodas, well above a typical cola. It's far below [Mtn Dew Kickstart]({{ '/caffeine-in/mtn-dew-kickstart/' | relative_url }}), the energy-drink-adjacent line built on the same brand, which carries considerably more per can.

## Diet sodas aren't uniformly lower

It's a common assumption that "diet" means less caffeine, but that isn't generally true: diet colas are often close to, or even above, their regular counterparts, the same pattern seen with generic [diet cola]({{ '/caffeine-in/diet-cola/' | relative_url }}) running higher than regular Coca-Cola Classic. Checking the actual label, rather than assuming from the word "diet," is the only reliable approach.

Mountain Dew's citrus flavour tends to mask caffeine's bitterness more than a cola does, which can make it easy to drink several cans without the taste signaling how much caffeine has been consumed.

## Where it fits in a day

At 54 mg, a can is far under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Seven cans, 378 mg, would come close to the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, three cans still fit under the day's allowance.
