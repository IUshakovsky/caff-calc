## Decaffeinated, not caffeine-free

Starbucks lists 15 mg of caffeine for a Short Decaf Pike Place Roast, 20 mg for a Tall, 25 mg for a Grande, and 30 mg for a Venti. That is a small fraction of regular [Pike Place Roast]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-pike-place-roast/' | relative_url }}), which carries 315 to 390 mg in a Grande, but it is not zero. Decaffeination removes most of coffee's caffeine, not all of it, which matches the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much)'s general note that decaffeinated coffees and teas "still contain some caffeine."

For general background on decaf across brands, see [caffeine in decaf coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/decaf-coffee/' | relative_url }}), which covers USDA's home-brewed figures alongside Starbucks' own.

## Where those 25 mg land

A Grande decaf carries about as much caffeine as a Grande [Caffè Misto]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caffe-misto/' | relative_url }}) has in roughly a sixth of its dose, and less than a Grande Starbucks hot chocolate. For most healthy adults working to the FDA's 400 mg daily figure, twelve Venti decafs would fit in a single day; this is not a drink that meaningfully counts against a normal limit.

## When it still matters

Three situations make the small number worth tracking. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, decaf is one of the easiest swaps to make, though several large decafs a day are not literally zero. Anyone told to avoid caffeine completely, for a medical reason or a sensitivity, should know that decaf is a reduction, not an elimination. And late at night, even 25 to 30 mg adds a little to whatever earlier coffee is still circulating, given caffeine's mean half-life of about five hours ([NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK223808/)).
