## More caffeine than the regular version

USDA's figure for a generic diet cola with caffeine is 43 mg in a 12 fl oz can, rising to 57 mg in a 16 fl oz can. That is more caffeine than regular [Coca-Cola Classic]({{ '/caffeine-in/coca-cola-classic/' | relative_url }}) at the same size (34 mg), and close to [Diet Coke]({{ '/caffeine-in/diet-coke/' | relative_url }}) specifically (46 mg). Manufacturers add caffeine and sugar independently, so cutting sugar for a diet formula doesn't reduce, and can even come with more, caffeine.

## A generic figure, not one brand

This page covers USDA's broad average across diet colas; specific brands vary; [Diet Coke]({{ '/caffeine-in/diet-coke/' | relative_url }}) and other named products may differ slightly from this generic figure, so check the can for an exact number when it matters. Against regular [cola]({{ '/caffeine-in/cola-soda/' | relative_url }}) generically (33 mg in a 12 oz can by USDA's figure), the diet version again comes out a little ahead.

## Why some diet sodas add more caffeine

One reason diet colas sometimes carry more caffeine than the regular version is that caffeine itself has a slightly bitter edge that can help balance the taste of artificial sweeteners, which read differently on the palate than sugar does. That's a plausible explanation for the pattern, not a rule every diet soda follows, since formulations vary by brand and country.

## Where it fits in a day

At 43 mg, a can is far under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Nine cans, 387 mg, would come close to the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, four cans still fit under the day's allowance.
