## A home or diner latte scales with the cup

USDA's food survey data puts a latte at 36 mg of caffeine per 100 g, which comes out to 86 mg in an 8 fl oz cup, 130 mg in a 12 fl oz Small, 173 mg in a 16 fl oz Medium and 216 mg in a 20 fl oz Large. Because this figure is a survey average across however lattes are actually made and poured, it scales straight with volume: a bigger cup means proportionally more coffee and more caffeine, the same way a bigger mug of [drip coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/brewed-coffee/' | relative_url }}) does.

That is a different rule from a coffee chain, where a latte is espresso and steamed milk in a fixed shot count. A Grande [Starbucks Caffè Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caffe-latte/' | relative_url }}) carries 150 mg regardless of the milk on top, and a Venti hot latte carries exactly the same 150 mg as a Grande, because both get two shots. So a 12 oz Small by USDA's figures (130 mg) and a 16 oz Grande at a chain (150 mg) are closer than the size difference implies, and a home-poured 20 oz Large (216 mg) can outrun a chain's biggest hot cup.

## Against cappuccino and coffee

A latte and a [cappuccino]({{ '/caffeine-in/cappuccino/' | relative_url }}) share the same USDA figure, 36 mg per 100 g, since both start from the same shot-to-milk ratio in the survey data; the milk foam changes texture, not caffeine. Both sit below an equivalent cup of drip coffee: an 8 oz cup of coffee at USDA's reference strength has 95 mg, more than an 8 oz latte (86 mg).

## Fitting it into a day

A 12 oz Small latte at 130 mg uses less than a third of the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults, leaving room for two more without approaching the limit. A 20 oz Large at 216 mg is the one size here that passes the 200 mg [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as a safe single dose for healthy adults, so treat that size as a full dose rather than a casual top-up.

Ordered in the afternoon, even a Small latte still carries real caffeine: with a mean half-life of about five hours ([NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK223808/)), roughly half of its 130 mg is still active five hours later, which matters if evening sleep is a concern.
