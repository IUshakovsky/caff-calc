## Slightly less than a plain latte

USDA's survey figure for café mocha is 33 mg of caffeine per 100 g, just under the 36 mg per 100 g it gives a [latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/latte/' | relative_url }}) or [cappuccino]({{ '/caffeine-in/cappuccino/' | relative_url }}). That comes to 82 mg in an 8 fl oz cup, 123 mg in a 12 fl oz Small, 164 mg in a 16 fl oz Medium and 205 mg in a 20 fl oz Large. The gap is mostly volume, not chocolate: chocolate syrup and cocoa add weight and sweetness to the cup, which slightly dilutes the caffeine per 100 g compared with a mocha's plain-latte cousins, even with the same shots underneath.

Chocolate itself contributes very little here. USDA measures unsweetened [cocoa powder](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/169593/nutrients) at far higher caffeine density than a finished mocha, but a mocha uses only a spoonful of syrup or powder per cup, so almost all of the drink's caffeine still comes from its espresso, not its chocolate.

## Against a Starbucks-style mocha

Coffee chains build a mocha the same way they build a latte, from a fixed shot count rather than a scaling ratio. A Grande [Starbucks Caffè Mocha](https://www.starbucks.com/menu/product/408/hot/nutrition) carries 175 mg by Starbucks' own published nutrition data, more than the 16 oz USDA figure for a home or diner mocha (164 mg), since Starbucks' espresso runs stronger per shot than the survey average and its mocha sauce adds a little caffeine of its own.

## Where it sits against your daily total

A Small mocha at 123 mg is well under a third of the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. The Large, 205 mg, is the size that edges past the 200 mg [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as a safe single dose for healthy adults, by just 5 mg, so it sits right at that line rather than clearly over it.

Pair it with food that also carries caffeine and the total moves fast: a Medium mocha (164 mg) and an ounce of [dark chocolate]({{ '/caffeine-in/dark-chocolate-70-85/' | relative_url }}) (23 mg) come to 187 mg, still under the EFSA single-dose figure but worth noting if anything else caffeinated is still to come that day.
