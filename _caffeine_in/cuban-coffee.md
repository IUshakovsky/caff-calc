## Sweet, strong, and small

Cafecito, Cuban coffee, is espresso brewed through sugar, which whips into a foam called espuma on top, then usually shared in small shots. USDA measures it at 199 mg of caffeine per 100 g, close to its figure for plain [espresso]({{ '/caffeine-in/espresso/' | relative_url }}) (212 mg per 100 g). A single 1 oz pour carries about 62 mg; the more typical 2 oz serving, the size a cafecito is often split from, carries about 123 mg.

The sugar changes the taste completely but does not dilute the caffeine much: cafecito lands close to an 8 oz cup of [drip coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/brewed-coffee/' | relative_url }}) (95 mg) in a fraction of the volume, and a shared pot poured into several shots gives each drinker a strong, quick dose.

## How it compares to other small, strong coffees

Cuban coffee sits in similar territory to other concentrated styles measured by USDA: [Turkish coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/turkish-coffee/' | relative_url }}) at 37 mg per 100 g is actually much weaker per gram, because it is served more diluted, while straight espresso is somewhat stronger per gram than cafecito. A cortadito, cafecito cut with steamed milk, carries the same caffeine as the cafecito it's made from; the milk changes the volume, not the dose.

## Counting cafecito against your day

At about 123 mg for a 2 oz serving, cafecito is under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults, though a colada, the larger shareable pour some Cuban coffee shops offer, can add up fast if drunk alone. Three 2 oz servings, 369 mg, approach the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults.
