## Blended coffee, a moderate dose

The Caramel Frappuccino carries 60 mg of caffeine in a Tall, 90 mg in a Grande, and 120 mg in a Venti. That's a little below the plain [Coffee Frappuccino]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-coffee-frappuccino/' | relative_url }}) at the same sizes (65, 95 and 125 mg), likely because caramel syrup and whipped topping take up some of the cup that would otherwise be coffee base.

Either way, a Frappuccino carries far less caffeine than an espresso drink of the same size: a Grande Caramel Frappuccino's 90 mg is well under a Grande [Caffè Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caffe-latte/' | relative_url }}) (150 mg).

## Against its chocolate sibling

The [Mocha Frappuccino]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-mocha-frappuccino/' | relative_url }}) runs a little higher (100 mg in a Grande), since the mocha sauce contributes its own small amount of caffeine on top of the coffee base, similar to how a hot mocha outpaces a hot latte.

## A dessert drink with a coffee habit's dose

Because a Frappuccino reads as a dessert drink more than a coffee, it's easy to underestimate how much caffeine is actually in it; a Grande's 90 mg is a genuine, countable dose, not an incidental amount. Anyone ordering one alongside a normal day's coffee should add it to the running total rather than treating it as caffeine-adjacent rather than caffeine-containing.

## Where it fits in a day

At 90 mg, a Grande is well under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Four Grandes, 360 mg, would come close to the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, two Grandes use most of the day's allowance.
