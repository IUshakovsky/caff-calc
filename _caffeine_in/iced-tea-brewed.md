## Cold, but not weaker than hot

Home-brewed unsweetened black iced tea carries 20 mg of caffeine per 100 g by USDA's figures, which works out to about 72 mg in a small 12 fl oz glass and 99 mg in a larger 16.5 fl oz glass. Per ounce, that is close to hot [black tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/black-tea/' | relative_url }}) (47 mg in an 8 oz cup, about 6 mg an ounce), since iced tea is typically just hot-brewed tea poured over ice or cold-steeped at a similar strength; the temperature doesn't remove caffeine.

## Homemade versus bottled

This figure is for tea brewed at home or in a restaurant from real leaves or bags. [Bottled iced tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/bottled-iced-tea/' | relative_url }}) from the grocery aisle runs weaker per ounce, about 9 mg per 100 g, because it's often diluted and blended for a milder taste. Starbucks' [Iced Black Tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-iced-black-tea/' | relative_url }}) sits in between, at 25 to 30 mg in a Grande.

Sweet tea, common in the American South, uses the same base and roughly the same caffeine; the sugar changes the taste, not the dose, the same pattern seen across sweetened and unsweetened versions of most caffeinated drinks.

## Where it fits in a day

A medium glass, 99 mg, is well under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Four medium glasses, 396 mg, come right up to the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, two medium glasses already use most of the day's allowance.
