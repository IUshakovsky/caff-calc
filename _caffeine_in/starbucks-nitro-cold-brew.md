## Nitrogen makes it creamy, not stronger

Nitro Cold Brew starts with Starbucks cold brew coffee that is infused with nitrogen gas and served from a tap. The nitrogen creates a cascade of tiny bubbles and a creamy, stout-like texture without adding any cream or sweetness. The caffeine comes entirely from the cold brew base.

Starbucks lists 215 mg of caffeine in a Tall (12 fl oz) and 280 mg in a Grande (16 fl oz). That makes a Grande Nitro Cold Brew 75 mg higher than a Grande regular Cold Brew, even though both are the same volume. The reason is simply that Starbucks brews the nitro version to a higher concentration; the nitrogen itself does not change the dose.

## A strong single drink

At 280 mg, one Grande Nitro Cold Brew is already 70% of the 400 mg daily reference the FDA cites for healthy adults. It is also above the 200 mg single-dose figure EFSA notes may affect sleep in some adults when taken close to bedtime. The per-ounce caffeine is high because the drink is served without ice and without milk to dilute it.

If you are ordering the larger sizes, note that Starbucks serves Nitro Cold Brew in Tall and Grande only; there is no Venti option because the nitrogen texture is designed to be consumed quickly while the cascade is still visible.

## Where the number comes from

The figures are taken from the [Starbucks published nutrition page for Nitro Cold Brew](https://www.starbucks.com/menu/product/2121912/iced/nutrition), checked 19 September 2026.
