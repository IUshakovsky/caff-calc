## Slightly stronger than the hot version

The iced Chai Latte, black tea infused with spices over ice with milk, carries 45 mg of caffeine in a Tall, 60 mg in a Grande, and 90 mg in a 24 oz Venti. That runs a little higher than the [hot Chai Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-chai-latte/' | relative_url }}) at the same sizes (35 mg Tall, 55 mg Grande, 75 mg Venti), likely down to how the chai concentrate is proportioned over ice.

Either way, chai stays well below coffee: a Grande iced chai's 60 mg is a fraction of a Grande [Caffè Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caffe-latte/' | relative_url }}) (150 mg) or a Grande [Pike Place Roast]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-pike-place-roast/' | relative_url }}) at the top of its range (390 mg).

## Against the other iced tea drinks

A Grande iced chai's 60 mg lands close to a Grande [Iced Matcha Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-iced-matcha-latte/' | relative_url }}) (65 mg) and above a Grande [Iced Black Tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-iced-black-tea/' | relative_url }}) (25 to 30 mg), since chai is built from a more concentrated tea base than a plain brewed iced tea.

Because the difference between hot and iced chai is small in absolute terms, choosing between them is really a question of temperature preference rather than caffeine management.

## Where it fits in a day

At 60 mg, a Grande is far under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Six Grandes, 360 mg, would be needed to approach the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, three Grandes, 180 mg, still fit under the daily allowance.
