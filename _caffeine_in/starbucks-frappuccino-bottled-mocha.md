## Grocery-aisle Frappuccino, different from the counter drink

The ready-to-drink bottled Frappuccino, sold in grocery stores and made by PepsiCo under license, is a different product from the blended drink Starbucks makes in its cafés. A 13.7 fl oz bottle of the Mocha flavour carries 105 mg of caffeine, close to a Grande in-store [Coffee Frappuccino]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-coffee-frappuccino/' | relative_url }}) (95 mg in 16 oz), despite the smaller bottle.

Per ounce, the bottled version runs a bit stronger than the blended café drink, and much weaker than a hot [Caffè Mocha]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caffe-mocha/' | relative_url }}) (175 mg in a Grande), which is built from real espresso shots rather than a coffee-based concentrate.

## One of several bottled Starbucks drinks

PepsiCo makes several ready-to-drink Starbucks products at different strengths, including the more concentrated [Espresso & Cream can]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-espresso-and-cream-can/' | relative_url }}) (120 mg in just 6.5 oz). Checking the specific bottle's label matters, since flavours and formulations can differ in caffeine.

Because it's shelf-stable and sold cold, it's a common grab-and-go substitute for a café visit, and worth remembering that a bottle's caffeine, while real, doesn't match a freshly made café Frappuccino of a comparable size exactly, since the two are made by different companies to different recipes.

## Where it fits in a day

At 105 mg, a bottle sits well under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Three bottles, 315 mg, still stay under the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, one bottle uses about half the day's allowance.
