## Lighter than a hot cup, size for size

Starbucks' Iced Black Tea carries 20 to 25 mg in a Tall, 25 to 30 mg in a Grande, 40 to 45 mg in a Venti, and 55 to 60 mg in a Trenta. That's notably lighter per ounce than its own hot brewed teas, which hold a flat 40 mg at every size, and lighter than home-brewed [iced tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/iced-tea-brewed/' | relative_url }}), which USDA puts at about 72 mg for a 12 oz glass; Starbucks' iced tea is brewed to be served over ice rather than a full-strength brew simply chilled.

## Matched by the green tea version

Starbucks' [Iced Green Tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-iced-green-tea/' | relative_url }}) publishes the identical ranges at every size, 20 to 25 mg in a Tall up to 55 to 60 mg in a Trenta, so the choice between the two comes down to taste rather than caffeine.

Requesting the tea steeped stronger, or extra tea bags, isn't a standard modification the way an extra espresso shot is, so these published figures are the reliable ones to work from rather than an estimate based on taste.

## Where it fits in a day

At up to 30 mg, a Grande is far under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Even a Trenta at the top of its range, 60 mg, is a small fraction of the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, several Grandes a day still fit comfortably.
