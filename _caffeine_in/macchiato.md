## A shot with a dash of milk

"Macchiato" means stained: a shot of espresso marked with a small amount of foamed milk, served in a 2 oz cup. USDA's figure for this style is 142 mg of caffeine per 100 g, which works out to about 85 mg in a typical 60 g cup, close to a single shot of [espresso]({{ '/caffeine-in/espresso/' | relative_url }}) on its own (63 mg by USDA's separate measurement, 75 mg for Starbucks' version).

That makes a macchiato one of the more concentrated ways to drink coffee. It carries almost as much caffeine as a full 8 oz mug of [drip coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/brewed-coffee/' | relative_url }}) (95 mg), in about a quarter of the volume.

## Not the same drink at every counter

The name covers two different drinks. A traditional or "espresso" macchiato, the one this page measures, is mostly espresso. Starbucks' own [Espresso Macchiato]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-espresso-macchiato/' | relative_url }}) follows that pattern too, at 75 mg for a single shot. Its better-known [Caramel Macchiato]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caramel-macchiato/' | relative_url }}), by contrast, is built more like a latte with the espresso poured on top, and carries 150 mg in a Grande, nearly double. Ordering "a macchiato" gets different drinks in different places, so check which one is meant.

## Where it fits in a day

At 85 mg, a traditional macchiato sits comfortably under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults, and four of them, 340 mg, would still leave room under the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, two cups already use most of that allowance.
