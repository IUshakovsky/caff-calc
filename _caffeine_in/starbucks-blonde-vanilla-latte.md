## Stronger shots, same shot count

A Blonde Vanilla Latte uses the same number of shots as a regular hot [Caffè Latte]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-caffe-latte/' | relative_url }}), one in a Short or Tall, two in a Grande or Venti, but pulls them from blonde espresso instead of Starbucks' signature roast. Blonde shots carry 85 mg each against 75 mg for signature, so the totals come out higher at every size: 85 mg in a Short or Tall, 170 mg in a Grande or Venti, 20 mg more than the same-size regular latte.

## Where blonde shots show up

Blonde espresso is the same shot used in Starbucks' [Cortado]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-cortado/' | relative_url }}) and, pulled ristretto, its [Flat White]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-flat-white/' | relative_url }}) (195 mg in a Grande). Ordering any Starbucks espresso drink "with blonde" instead of the default roast adds roughly 10 mg per shot.

## Blonde as a taste choice, not a strength choice

Baristas sometimes describe blonde espresso as "milder" or "smoother," which refers to the roasting profile and flavour, not the caffeine; on caffeine alone, blonde is the stronger option of the two. Someone choosing blonde purely for a lighter taste should know they're getting slightly more caffeine along with it, not less, which runs against the usual assumption that lighter automatically means milder in every sense.

## Where it fits in a day

At 170 mg, a Grande sits under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Two Grandes, 340 mg, come close to the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, a Grande alone uses most of the day's allowance.
