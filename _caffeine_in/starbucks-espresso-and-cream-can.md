## A small can with a real dose

Starbucks Espresso & Cream, a ready-to-drink can made by PepsiCo, packs 120 mg of caffeine into a small 6.5 fl oz can. Per ounce, that is considerably stronger than a generic [bottled coffee drink]({{ '/caffeine-in/bottled-coffee/' | relative_url }}) (about 28 mg per 100 g) and closer to straight [Starbucks Espresso]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-espresso/' | relative_url }}), which is exactly what it's built to imitate in a shelf-stable can.

## Easy to finish in one go

Because the can is small, it's easy to drink the whole 120 mg quickly, unlike a larger iced coffee that might be sipped over an hour. That is worth knowing if you're also having espresso drinks or brewed coffee the same day: the can's dose is comparable to a Doppio (two shots) of [Starbucks Espresso]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-espresso/' | relative_url }}) at 150 mg, just slightly less.

## A convenience product, not a café substitute

This can is designed for grab-and-go convenience rather than replicating the café experience exactly; the espresso is combined with cream and packaged for shelf stability, which changes the texture and flavour from a freshly pulled shot even though the caffeine dose is comparable. It's a reasonable stand-in for a quick shot of caffeine when a café isn't an option.

## Where it fits in a day

At 120 mg, a can sits well under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Three cans, 360 mg, stay under the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, one can uses well over half the day's allowance.
