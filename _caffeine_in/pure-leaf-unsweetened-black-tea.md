## Stronger than the average bottled tea

Pure Leaf Unsweetened Black Tea carries 54 mg of caffeine in its 18.5 fl oz bottle, by PepsiCo's own figures. Per ounce, that runs a bit ahead of the generic [bottled iced tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/bottled-iced-tea/' | relative_url }}) average (about 9 mg per 100 g, or roughly 45 mg in a 16 oz bottle), likely because it's brewed from real leaves without dilution for sweetness, unlike many blended bottled teas.

That puts it closer to home-brewed [iced tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/iced-tea-brewed/' | relative_url }}), which USDA measures at about 72 mg for a 12 oz glass, than to the weaker end of the bottled category.

## A real leaf, not a blend

"Unsweetened" and "Pure Leaf" both point at the same thing here: this is closer to a straight-brewed tea than many bottled competitors that dilute the tea to balance added sugar. That's consistent with USDA's figure for home-brewed [black tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/black-tea/' | relative_url }}), 47 mg for an 8 oz cup, scaling up reasonably to this bottle's 18.5 oz size.

Pure Leaf makes other flavours and a sweetened line too; this figure is specific to the unsweetened black tea bottle, and a different Pure Leaf product may carry a different amount, so checking the specific label is worth doing if the number matters.

## Where it fits in a day

At 54 mg, a bottle is far under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. Seven bottles, 378 mg, would come close to the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, three bottles still fit under the day's allowance.
