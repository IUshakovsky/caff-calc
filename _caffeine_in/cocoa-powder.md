## The most concentrated form of chocolate's caffeine

USDA measures unsweetened cocoa powder at 230 mg of caffeine per 100 g, by far the highest figure of any chocolate product on a per-gram basis, since it's pure cocoa solids with no cocoa butter, milk or sugar diluting it. A single tablespoon (5.4 g) still carries about 12 mg, close to what an ounce of [70 to 85% dark chocolate]({{ '/caffeine-in/dark-chocolate-70-85/' | relative_url }}) carries (about 23 mg), in a fraction of the weight.

## Where it ends up

Most cocoa powder is used a tablespoon or two at a time, in baking or a mug of homemade hot chocolate, so the caffeine per serving stays modest even though the per-gram figure is high. A homemade hot chocolate made with two tablespoons of cocoa powder carries roughly the same caffeine as a Grande [Starbucks Hot Chocolate]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-hot-chocolate/' | relative_url }}) (25 mg), depending on how much powder goes in.

Dutch-processed and natural cocoa powders differ in acidity and flavour but aren't reported separately for caffeine by USDA, so this figure is a reasonable estimate across both styles rather than a distinction the data draws.

## Where it fits in a day

A tablespoon, 12 mg, is a small fraction of the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. Even a generous baking recipe using a quarter cup of cocoa powder, about 50 mg, stays well under it. It matters most for children, given the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much)'s note that caffeine naturally present in an ingredient like cocoa doesn't have to appear separately on a label.
