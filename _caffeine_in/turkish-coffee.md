## A strong brew served in a small cup

Turkish coffee is unfiltered, finely ground coffee simmered in a small pot (a cezve) and served unstrained, grounds and all, in a demitasse cup far smaller than a standard mug. USDA reports it at 37 mg of caffeine per 100 g, which scales to 92 mg for a full 8 fl oz, USDA's standard reporting size. But a traditional serving is only 2 to 3 fl oz, so the actual cup carries something closer to 23 to 35 mg, not the full 92 mg figure.

Per fluid ounce brewed, that puts it below [drip coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/brewed-coffee/' | relative_url }}) (about 12 mg an ounce) and far below [espresso]({{ '/caffeine-in/espresso/' | relative_url }}) (around 63 mg an ounce by USDA's figure), because the grounds simmer rather than being forced through under pressure.

## Small cup, sometimes several rounds

Because the serving is small, it is common to drink more than one round over a visit or a meal. Three demitasse cups at the low end, about 70 mg total, sit well under a single 8 oz mug of drip coffee. Someone drinking a full 8 fl oz worth across several refills, 92 mg by USDA's figure, is closer to that mug.

## Where it fits in a day

Even a generous run of Turkish coffee stays under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults, and it takes roughly four full 8 fl oz equivalents, close to 370 mg, to approach the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults. The grounds left in the cup are not swallowed, so they don't add to the dose.
