## Same shots, stronger roast

This drink uses the identical shot counts as Starbucks' [Iced Shaken Espresso]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-iced-shaken-espresso/' | relative_url }}), two shots in a Tall, three in a Grande, four in a Venti, but pulls them from blonde espresso, which Starbucks lists at 85 mg a shot against 75 mg for the signature roast. That difference alone accounts for the gap: 170 mg in a Tall, 255 mg in a Grande, and 340 mg in a Venti, each 20 to 30 mg above the plain version at the same size.

The brown sugar syrup and oatmilk add flavour and creaminess but no caffeine of their own; the entire increase over the standard shaken espresso comes from the roast, not the other ingredients.

## One of the stronger iced drinks on the menu

At 255 mg, a Grande carries more caffeine than a Grande [Cold Brew]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-cold-brew/' | relative_url }}) (205 mg) and close to a Grande [Nitro Cold Brew]({{ '/caffeine-in/starbucks-nitro-cold-brew/' | relative_url }}) (280 mg), despite being an espresso-based drink rather than steeped coffee.

Because the extra caffeine comes from the roast rather than an added shot, asking for signature espresso instead of blonde in this drink brings it back down to the plain Iced Shaken Espresso's numbers, without changing the brown sugar or oatmilk.

## Where it fits in a day

A Grande, 255 mg, is over the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. A Venti, 340 mg, is most of the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults, on its own. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, even a Tall uses most of the day's allowance.
