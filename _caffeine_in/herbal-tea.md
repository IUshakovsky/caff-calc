## Not tea at all, botanically

"Herbal tea" is a catch-all for infusions made from herbs, flowers or fruit, like chamomile and peppermint, rather than from *Camellia sinensis*, the plant that produces black, green, white and oolong tea. USDA's figure for herbal tea brewed from a bag is 0 mg of caffeine per 8 fl oz cup, because there's no actual tea leaf involved.

That makes it a genuine zero, unlike [decaf black tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/decaf-black-tea/' | relative_url }}), which still carries a couple of milligrams because it starts from real tea leaves and removes most, not all, of the caffeine.

## Check the blend

The catch is that "herbal tea" isn't a protected term, and some blends marketed that way include real tea leaves, yerba mate, or guayusa, all of which do contain caffeine. A "herbal chai" or a fruit blend cut with black tea will carry real caffeine despite the herbal branding. Reading the ingredient list is the only reliable check; if actual tea, mate or guayusa is listed, the drink is not caffeine-free.

## Why it matters

For anyone avoiding caffeine entirely, whether for pregnancy, a sensitivity, or an evening cutoff, a genuine single-herb infusion like chamomile or peppermint is one of the few drinks that adds nothing to the day's total. It is also a safe late-night alternative to [black tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/black-tea/' | relative_url }}) (47 mg an 8 oz cup) or [green tea]({{ '/caffeine-in/green-tea/' | relative_url }}) (about 29 mg), given caffeine's mean half-life of roughly five hours ([NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK223808/)), which keeps real tea circulating well into the evening if drunk after dinner.
