## Caffeine as a diuretic, not the headline ingredient

Midol Complete combines acetaminophen, a pain reliever, with pyrilamine maleate, a diuretic antihistamine, and caffeine, which the drug label itself lists as a diuretic ingredient rather than a stimulant. Each gelcap carries 60 mg, and the standard two-gelcap dose comes to 120 mg, a little more than an 8 oz cup of [drip coffee]({{ '/caffeine-in/brewed-coffee/' | relative_url }}) at USDA's reference strength (95 mg).

## The daily maximum adds up fast

The label allows up to six gelcaps in 24 hours, which comes to 360 mg of caffeine, close to the 400 mg daily figure the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much) cites for healthy adults, before counting any coffee, tea or soda taken the same day. Someone treating cramps with Midol across a full day, plus a normal morning coffee, can reach or pass that daily figure without realizing caffeine is part of the medicine.

## Compared to other OTC caffeine

At 60 mg a gelcap, Midol's caffeine sits below [Excedrin]({{ '/caffeine-in/excedrin-extra-strength/' | relative_url }}) (65 mg a caplet) and above [Anacin]({{ '/caffeine-in/anacin/' | relative_url }}) (32 mg a tablet), all of which list caffeine for a different stated purpose on the label, whether as a pain reliever aid or, in Midol's case, a diuretic.

## Where it fits against a single dose

A two-gelcap dose, 120 mg, is under the 200 mg single dose [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/4102) assessed as safe for healthy adults. In pregnancy, where [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2010/08/moderate-caffeine-consumption-during-pregnancy) uses a 200 mg daily ceiling, one dose already uses most of the day's allowance; check with a doctor before using any OTC medicine for menstrual symptoms during pregnancy.
